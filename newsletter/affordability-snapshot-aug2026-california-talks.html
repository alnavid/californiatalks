Why does everything cost more here?

Dear Neighbor,

If it feels like everything costs more these days, you’re not imagining it—and you’re not alone. New research from the Public Policy Institute of California confirms what families across California have been saying: the cost of living is the defining economic challenge facing our state. Here’s the data behind the squeeze.

And housing isn’t the only pressure: families now spend 6–28% of their income on child care for young children, and health insurance premiums have grown at three times the rate of workers’ earnings over the past 25 years. Over the past year, the average California worker’s inflation-adjusted wage actually fell 0.2%.

Both sides of the equation

Affordability comes down to two things: what things cost, and what families earn. Lasting solutions have to work on both. That means clearing the regulatory and permitting delays that slow down homebuilding and job creation, investing in the skills and training that help Californians earn more, and protecting the safety net for families struggling hardest—rather than reaching for quick fixes that research shows can make affordability worse over time.

Behind every one of these numbers is a family making hard tradeoffs at the kitchen table. California Talks wants to understand which pressures are hitting households hardest—whether housing, child care, health care, energy bills, or something else. What Californians share helps us ask better questions and report public opinion more clearly.
